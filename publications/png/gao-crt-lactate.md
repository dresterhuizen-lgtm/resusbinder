## CLINICAL QUESTION
Does resuscitation targeting capillary refill time (CRT) use less fluid than a lactate-targeted protocol in adult septic shock?
## STUDY SNAPSHOT
- Single-centre, open-label randomized study, 85 patients with septic shock.
- Six-hour resuscitation targeting CRT of 3 seconds or less versus lactate normalization or a specified decrease.
- Fluid administered during early resuscitation was emphasized in subsequent reporting.
## ACTUAL RESULTS
- Six-hour total IV input: 1135 mL (IQR 824-1480) with CRT versus 1350 mL (990-1850) with lactate guidance.
- Estimated difference was approximately 250 mL less in the CRT group.
- No reliable demonstration of improvement in mortality or other patient-centred outcomes.
## CRITICAL APPRAISAL
- Trial was under-recruited for the original mortality-driven sample-size calculation.
- Retrospective registration and changes in outcome-reporting emphasis limit confidence.
- Results are exploratory: a fluid-volume difference is not proof of improved tissue perfusion or clinical benefit.
## PHYSIOLOGY REVIEW
- CRT is rapidly repeatable and reflects peripheral perfusion, affected by temperature, vasomotor tone and measurement technique.
- Lactate reflects production and clearance; it is neither a pure hypoxia marker nor an automatic fluid trigger.
- Neither abnormal CRT nor raised lactate independently identifies low preload, vasoplegia, LV failure or venous congestion.
## RESUSCITATION IMPLICATION
- Use CRT serially alongside mentation, skin perfusion, lactate trend, MAP, dynamic flow testing and POCUS.
- A prolonged CRT prompts reassessment, not a mandatory fluid bolus.
- Stronger context: ANDROMEDA-SHOCK-2 supports a personalized CRT-targeted strategy, not CRT as a stand-alone treatment instruction.
