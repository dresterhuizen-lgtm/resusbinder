## THE CLINICAL QUESTION
Which induction agent best preserves haemodynamics during emergency rapid-sequence intubation, especially in shock?
## EVIDENCE REVIEW
- McDougall et al., network meta-analysis: 21 randomized trials and 6031 critically ill patients.
- Ketamine versus etomidate: peri-intubation haemodynamic instability RR 1.31 (95% CI 1.15-1.48), moderate certainty.
- Ketamine-propofol comparisons are lower certainty and should not drive routine practice.
- Zampieri et al. (9 trials, 4672 patients) found no demonstrated mortality difference; cardiovascular collapse was more frequent with ketamine (OR 1.44, 95% CI 1.20-1.71).
- These reviews share underlying trials; do not treat them as independent patient populations.
## CRITICAL INTERPRETATION
- Etomidate generally offers more immediate circulatory stability but causes biochemical adrenal suppression.
- Ketamine has sympathomimetic effects, yet direct myocardial depression may be exposed in catecholamine-depleted shock.
- Neither drug removes the need for pre-induction haemodynamic optimization and post-intubation vigilance.
- The evidence does not establish a mortality advantage of one induction agent or an automatic dose for every shock phenotype.
## PHYSIOLOGICALLY DIFFICULT AIRWAY
- Induction reduces endogenous sympathetic support; positive-pressure ventilation can decrease venous return.
- RV failure, obstructive physiology, profound acidosis, hypoxaemia and haemorrhagic shock create distinct hazards.
- Avoid abruptly removing compensatory minute ventilation in severe metabolic acidosis.
## PRACTICAL RESUSCITATION SEQUENCE
- Before induction, assess phenotype and optimize oxygenation, perfusion and rescue airway plan as time permits.
- Have a vasopressor plan, laryngoscopy/bougie, supraglottic rescue and front-of-neck access equipment ready.
- Choose agent and dose to the patient's physiology. Avoid assuming ketamine is haemodynamically protective.
- After tube placement, confirm waveform capnography; immediately reassess BP, ETCO2, ventilation, sedation and perfusion.
