## CLINICAL QUESTION
Can a bedside safety algorithm using congestion and cardiac filling signals limit additional fluid boluses in initially fluid-responsive septic shock?
## STUDY SNAPSHOT
- Multicentre randomized trial, 36 randomized and 35 analysed; recruitment stopped early.
- Included ventilated septic-shock patients with persistent hypoperfusion, fluid responsiveness and risk of fluid intolerance.
- Intervention used lung ultrasound, VExUS, E/lateral e-prime and CVP together in a traffic-light fluid-bolus algorithm.
## ACTUAL RESULTS
- Protocol bolus fluid at six hours: median 250 mL versus 650 mL in control.
- Change in oxygenation (PaO2/FiO2) favoured intervention; the small prematurely stopped study makes the estimate uncertain.
- Total cumulative fluid exposure was similar; CRT and lactate changes did not significantly differ.
- There was no demonstrated mortality, renal recovery or other definitive patient-centred benefit.
## CRITICAL APPRAISAL
- Strength: directly tests the important distinction between responsiveness and tolerance.
- Limitation: tiny sample, early stopping, short-term surrogate endpoints, highly selected mechanically ventilated population.
- The intervention combined multiple observations: this does not validate isolated VExUS-based fluid decisions.
- Newsletter curator Philippe Rola was a study coauthor; disclose this when appraising the newsletter.
## PHYSIOLOGY REVIEW
- Fluid responsiveness asks whether greater preload increases stroke volume.
- Fluid tolerance asks whether fluid is likely to create unacceptable pulmonary or venous pressure consequences.
- High venous afterload can impair organ drainage despite acceptable MAP.
## RESUSCITATION IMPLICATION
- Before further fluid: establish a reason to augment flow and evaluate intolerance signals.
- Integrate cardiac/RV POCUS, lung B-lines, venous Doppler and clinical trajectory; do not treat a single waveform as a stop rule.
- Bottom line: physiologically compelling and hypothesis-generating, not a validated definitive stopping threshold.
